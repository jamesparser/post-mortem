# Requirements: Web Funeral

## Overview

A browser-based funeral for a dead device. A stranger opens the link, names or shows a device, hears an AI priest deliver a eulogy over organ and storm ambience, and watches flowers fall as the casket is lowered. Two minutes, no account, no install.

## User stories

### US-1. Name the device

As a mourner, I want to tell the app what died, so the eulogy is about my device and not a generic phone.

Acceptance:

- A single text field accepts a freeform description. Examples: "iPhone 12, cracked screen", "old Kindle from college", "my PS4 controller with stick drift".
- A photo option exists. I can capture from the webcam or upload an image.
- Submitting either path reaches the eulogy step. I can skip the photo.
- Empty submit shows a quiet inline prompt. No modal. No error styling that breaks the mood.

### US-2. Device recognition

As a mourner, I want the app to look at my device and know what it is, so the eulogy mentions the real damage and the real history.

Acceptance:

- A webcam capture or uploaded image is sent to a vision model.
- The response returns make, model, and visible condition as structured fields.
- Those fields appear in the eulogy text. Example: a cracked screen becomes "though his screen was cracked, his spirit was unbroken".
- If recognition fails or the user skips it, the typed description is used alone. The eulogy still works.
- No image leaves the browser except to our backend. The backend calls the vision model. Keys never reach the client.

### US-3. Write the eulogy

As a mourner, I want a eulogy that is sincere and quietly funny, so the experience lands.

Acceptance:

- The eulogy is 80 to 140 words. Long enough to feel real. Short enough to hold attention.
- It opens solemn. It ends solemn. The device facts do the comedy in the middle.
- It includes at least one specific detail from recognition or the typed description.
- It never uses the words "lol", "rip" in the internet sense, or emoji.
- It never mentions that it was generated by AI.
- It follows the house rules: no em dash, no en dash, no double hyphen. Space after period.

### US-4. Speak it aloud

As a mourner, I want a priest voice reading the eulogy, so the room feels like a service.

Acceptance:

- Deepgram Aura TTS renders the eulogy in a male voice.
- The audio plays over the organ and storm bed at a lower volume than the voice.
- Playback can be replayed and muted.
- The TTS call goes through the backend. The Deepgram key is an env var on the server.
- If TTS fails, the eulogy text is shown and the ambience keeps playing. The experience never hard fails.

### US-5. The service

As a mourner, I want the visual ritual, so it feels like a funeral and not a chatbot.

Acceptance:

- A casket sits in the center of the screen with the device name on a small plate.
- Flowers fall slowly across the scene for the duration of the eulogy.
- Storm and wind bed plays quietly from the start. Organ dirge fades in when the eulogy begins.
- After the audio ends, the casket lowers out of view on chains. The lowering itself is quiet and mechanical. No haptic, or only a faint settling thud. It must never read as a heartbeat.
- A beat of silence after the casket is gone. This silence is load bearing. Do not fill it.
- Then a heartbeat from the ground. Two soft pulses, a lub and a dub, at about 60 beats per minute, muffled as if coming up through soil. It repeats three or four times, then stops and does not come back.
- On phones that allow it, the heartbeat is the vibration. Two pulses, a pause, two pulses. Nothing else vibrates during the lowering, so the heartbeat is unmistakable.
- The heartbeat is not a resurrection. The device does not work again. It is the horror of something that will not stay quiet.
- The screen holds on the closed grave for a beat.
- A final line appears: "Rest now. He will not be asked to update one more time."
- A "Bury another" control returns to the start.

### US-6. Sound bed

As a mourner, I want the house to feel haunted, so silence is never empty.

Acceptance:

- Ambient bed is layered: storm, wind, distant footsteps on wood, occasional groans.
- Layers are loopable and gapless.
- The eulogy voice is ducked above the bed. Bed drops to about 20 percent under speech.
- I can mute all audio with one control that stays visible.

## Non-functional

- Works on a modern desktop browser and a modern phone browser.
- First meaningful paint under 2 seconds on a laptop.
- No login. No cookie banner beyond what the host requires.
- No analytics beyond basic uptime.
- Total static payload under 5 MB, audio excluded.

## Out of scope

- Resurrection of the device. It stays dead.
- Payments, accounts, sharing to social networks.
- Real time multiplayer funerals.
- Quest 3 features. Those live in the `thing-quest` spec.
